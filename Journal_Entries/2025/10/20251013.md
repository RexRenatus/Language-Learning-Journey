# Reflection

- The word frequency analysis reveals the natural learning progression: we've already mastered the atomic units (characters), now we need to learn how they combine into words before tackling full phrases. This is similar to learning letters → syllables → words → sentences in alphabetic languages.

- The 80/20 rule (Pareto Principle) is strongly validated in language learning: focusing on the top 25% of words (996 words) gives us 93% coverage. This suggests that after character mastery, targeting ~1,000 high-frequency words is the most efficient path before moving to full n-gram phrase study.

- Single-character words dominate frequency (70%) despite being the minority of unique words (20%). This confirms that our character flashcard foundation is critical—these single characters appear constantly and form the backbone of the language.

- The separation of words from n-grams clarifies our learning journey: characters are the building blocks, words are the modules, and n-grams are the complete structures. We can't effectively study n-gram phrases without first understanding the individual words that compose them.
