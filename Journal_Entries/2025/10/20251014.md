# Reflection

- The definition flashcard infrastructure closes the semantic gap in character learning. Without meaning anchoring, we risk creating pronunciation automatons—characters become sounds without comprehension. The triple-format approach (Form × Sound × Meaning) ensures we're building vocabulary, not just recognition skills.

- The 50/50/50 strategy represents a shift from sequential learning (master one format before the next) to parallel triple exposure. This mirrors natural language acquisition where we encounter words simultaneously through multiple modalities. Each character appears 3× daily in different contexts, building redundant neural pathways that reinforce retention through variation rather than repetition.

- The distractor selection algorithm using frequency proximity (±200 range) creates contextually appropriate difficulty. Characters at similar frequency levels appear in similar contexts, making wrong answers plausible rather than obviously incorrect. This forces genuine discrimination rather than process-of-elimination guessing. The prime offset randomization ensures consistent variety without predictable patterns.

- The decision to show character + pinyin for each choice (not just character alone) was critical. It reinforces the form-sound connection on every card, preventing the definition deck from becoming isolated semantic learning. The front card format "的 (de)" integrates all three dimensions: we see the definition, match it to the character, and simultaneously reinforce pinyin pronunciation.

- The 18-column CSV structure (vs original 13 columns) reflects this integration requirement. Each choice needs both visual (character) and phonetic (pinyin) components. The expanded structure adds complexity but ensures every flashcard exposure reinforces the complete Form × Sound × Meaning triangle.

- The 6-week timeline with 1-week checkpoint creates a feedback loop for calibration. If 150 cards/day (50/50/50) proves too aggressive, we can adjust to 120 (40/40/40) or 90 (30/30/30) without losing the triple-exposure benefit. The checkpoint prevents burnout from unsustainable pacing while maintaining momentum.
